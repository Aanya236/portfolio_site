Manifesto

A Manifesto for Slow Interiors

By Aanya Kaushik

Today, we are living in a world of "Fast Interiors." Just like fast fashion, we have fallen into a trap where furniture and home decor are treated as disposable items. We see a beautiful room on social media, we buy cheap versions of those items online, and a few years later when the trend changes or the leg breaks, we throw them away. Our homes have become temporary backdrops rather than meaningful places to live. This cycle of "buy and discard" is exhausting for the consumer and devastating for our world. We are surrounded by objects that have no soul, no history, and no future beyond the local landfill.

The Issue: The Disposable Home

The problem is that "fast design" is hollow. It relies on low-quality materials like particle board, toxic glues, and plastic that cannot be fixed once they break. These materials are designed to look good for a photo but fail under the pressure of real life. It ignores the environment, creating mountains of waste in our landfills. Every year, millions of tons of furniture are tossed aside simply because they weren't built to last more than a season.

But more importantly, this issue ignores the human spirit. When we fill our homes with things that have no history and no soul, we don't feel truly connected to our spaces. We live in rooms that feel like hotel suites — anonymous and replaceable. A home should be a collection of stories, not a collection of cardboard boxes delivered to your door. When every item in your room is a "temporary" solution, your life starts to feel temporary, too. We have lost the art of nesting and the patience required to build a home slowly and thoughtfully.

The Problem of Modern Materials

In contemporary design, we have become addicted to convenience. We want a room to look "finished" in a single weekend. To achieve this, designers and homeowners turn to mass-produced items that use massive amounts of timber. While wood is a natural material, the sheer volume of "fast furniture" being produced means we are cutting down trees at a rate that the earth cannot sustain. We are sacrificing our forests for coffee tables that will be replaced in three years. This is not just a design problem; it is a moral one. We must find a way to create beautiful, warm, and inviting spaces while minimising our impact on the natural world and protecting our trees.

My Philosophy: Quality Over Hype

As an interior designer, I refuse to be part of the "throwaway" cycle. I believe that a well-designed room should age with you. It should get better over time, not worse. I believe in the beauty of a scratch on a solid surface because it shows that a life was lived there. I believe that "new" is not always better than "old".

My philosophy is built on the idea of Slow Interiors. This means taking the time to choose the right piece rather than the fast piece. It means looking for materials that don't require the constant cutting of trees. It means valuing the work of a human hand over the output of a giant machine. I want to shift the focus from "how much can I buy?" to "how long can I keep this?"

How I Will Work

  • Choosing Real, Sustainable Materials: I will prioritise materials that last — stone, metal, and natural fabrics. I will minimise the use of new wood by looking for alternatives like bamboo, cork, or recycled materials that don't harm our forests. If it can't be repaired, it shouldn't be in the room.
  • Supporting the Local and the Hand-made: I will look for local makers and craftsmen. A chair made by a person in your community is worth more than ten chairs made in a giant factory. These pieces carry the energy and skill of the person who made them, adding a layer of soul to the home that a factory-made item can never replicate.
  • Designing for Evolution: I will design spaces that can change. A nursery today should be able to become a study tomorrow without needing to gut the whole room and start over. By planning for the future, we save money, time, and materials.
  • Encouraging "Slow" Decisions: I will encourage my clients to buy less, but buy better. It is better to have one empty corner for a year than to fill it with a cheap piece of furniture you will hate in six months. Patience is a design tool just as important as a colour palette.

My Declaration

My future practice will be about creating "Forever Homes". I am not interested in trends that expire in six months. I want to design interiors that people want to keep, care for, and eventually pass down. I choose to design for people, for the planet, and for the long haul. By choosing quality, minimising waste, and respecting our trees, we can create spaces that aren't just beautiful to look at, but beautiful to live in forever.
